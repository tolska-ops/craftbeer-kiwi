# Concept Snapshot: NZ Brewery Third-Party Supplier Landscape

**One-liner:** A survey of who NZ breweries actually pay for goods and services — ingredients, packaging, equipment, insurance, distribution — sourced primarily from the Brewers Guild of NZ's own supplier directory, captured as market-research reference rather than anything actioned in the build.

**Why this exists:** Prompted by general market-research curiosity (29 July) about where NZ breweries' money goes, partly with an eye on whether industry-facing sponsorship (suppliers paying for visibility to breweries, the way they already do via the Guild) is a viable future revenue model for craftbeer.kiwi, alongside the existing consumer-facing distribution-plan thinking. Captured here so it doesn't only live in chat history.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** This is one research session, not exhaustive. The Brewers Guild of NZ supplier directory only lists *paying corporate members*, so it skews toward larger/established suppliers and likely undercounts smaller regional players (e.g. Punky Distributions, a South Island craft-specific distributor found via a single brewery's own stockist page, not the Guild directory). Treat as directionally accurate, not a complete market map.

---

## Ingredients — malt, hops, yeast

The category with the most visible, recurring spend, split between NZ-grown and imported:

| Supplier | What they do | NZ-grown or imported |
|---|---|---|
| **Gladfield Malt** | NZ-grown, Canterbury-based maltster | NZ |
| **NZ Hops** | NZ-grown hops, Nelson-based | NZ |
| **Cryer Malt** | Malt/hops/yeast, principal sponsor of the NZ Beer Awards — clearly a dominant ingredients relationship for many NZ breweries | Imported |
| **Clayton Hops**, **Hop Products Australia (HPA)** | Hop-specific suppliers, largely Australian/international varieties | Imported |
| **Fermentis** (Lesaffre), **Lallemand Brewing** | Global dry-yeast suppliers, both with dedicated ANZ reps | Imported |
| **Bintani** | Australian raw-materials distributor (malt/hops/adjuncts), one-stop-shop model | Imported |
| **Invita** | ANZ technical ingredients supplier, positions on brewing expertise/troubleshooting, not just product | Imported |

## Packaging & vessels

- **Kegstar**, **Konvoy Kegs** — keg *rental/pooling* services. A genuinely significant recurring cost for any brewery doing kegged trade: rather than owning a keg fleet outright, breweries rent from a shared pool — capital-efficient but ongoing.
- **MCC Label**, **Label & Litho (LM Group)** — can/bottle label printing.
- **PhilStic** — appears to sit in the same label/print space based on directory context, not independently confirmed.

## Hygiene, cleaning & lab/QC

- **Clark Products** — Hawke's Bay-based, 50+ years, industrial cleaning chemicals for food & beverage (confirmed generally, not brewery-specific).
- **Anton Paar** — lab instrumentation (density meters, spectrophotometers) for quality control — a capital purchase rather than recurring spend.

## Equipment

- **Viniquip** — NZ-based beverage equipment supplier: tanks, filtration, general brewhouse/packaging hardware.

## Insurance

- **ICIB** — an insurance broker specifically positioned to the brewing industry. Worth noting as its own line item — brewery insurance (liability, stock, business interruption) is a real, ongoing cost that consumer-facing directory research usually skips over entirely.

## Distribution & wholesale

Not covered by the Guild's own supplier directory, but arguably the single biggest spend category for any brewery not selling 100% direct from the taproom:

- **Hancocks** — large-scale NZ importer/distributor, exclusive NZ agent for many spirit/wine brands, also carries a select craft beer range.
- **Trents Wholesale** (Foodstuffs South Island subsidiary), **Federal Merchants & Co** — general liquor wholesale/distribution.
- **Green Drop** (formerly Turanga Creek) — notably positioned specifically for *organic and independent* producers, closer to craftbeer.kiwi's own audience than the big generalist distributors.
- Smaller regional/independent distributors exist too — e.g. 8 Wired uses **Punky Distributions** for South Island coverage specifically. A reminder that a lot of small NZ breweries route through boutique regional distributors rather than the big three, and that this layer is genuinely under-documented compared to ingredients/packaging.

## Education

- **Lincoln University** — listed as a Guild supplier-directory member via its brewing/fermentation science programme. A training-pipeline data point rather than a vendor cost, but relevant to where the industry invests.

## Not verified — genuinely unresolved

**POS/taproom software.** Square, Lightspeed/Vend, H&L, and Impos are all heavily used across NZ hospitality generally, but no NZ-brewery-specific source confirmed which platform (if any) dominates specifically among breweries. This category appears genuinely fragmented rather than having one clear leader like the ingredients/keg space does. If this needs sizing properly, it would require direct outreach to a handful of breweries rather than further desk research — no public data source is likely to answer it reliably.

---

## Distilled pattern

Two categories stand out as having a clear dominant/consolidated player (Cryer Malt in ingredients, Kegstar/Konvoy in keg rental), while distribution and POS software both look genuinely fragmented — no single winner, several regional or generalist players splitting the market. Worth remembering this asymmetry if this research ever gets used for anything: "who's the biggest supplier" is a confidently answerable question in some categories and a genuinely open one in others.

## Possible relevance to craftbeer.kiwi (not actioned)

The Brewers Guild's own supplier-directory model — vendors paying for visibility to breweries — is a live example of the same "sell to the industry, not just the consumer" approach The Crafty Pint uses via tiered listings (see `craftbeer-kiwi-concept-benchmarks.md`). Not suggesting this gets built now — craftbeer.kiwi doesn't have the traffic to make a sponsorship pitch credible yet — but worth keeping as a second real-world data point if industry-facing monetisation is ever revisited alongside the existing consumer-distribution thinking in `todo.md`.
